How the observed language differs

The tables separate issue wording from opponent portrayals. Read the contextual examples below to determine who is speaking and who is being described. Primary attacks on same-party candidates and positive self-descriptions are not evidence of cross-party attacks. No semantic attack rate is estimated.

The saved Democratic-aligned messages include special-interest wording in 226/1194 (18.9%) of messages, compared with 21/451 (4.7%) on the Republican side. Extreme/radical wording occurs in 40/451 (8.9%) of Republican-aligned messages and 6/1194 (0.5%) of Democratic-aligned messages. These are whole-text phrase matches, not counts of independently verified opponent descriptions.

How to read this report

  • This is an observed corpus of official ad releases, reviewed campaign originals, Google previews and native Snap creatives, not a census of all ads aired.
  • Dates distinguish publisher releases/posts, Google-reported delivery and Snap scheduled intervals. Snap's positive impressions do not independently locate delivery within its scheduled dates.
  • Party alignment describes the source publisher; joint creative payers require separate review.
  • Reviewed publisher alignment uses documented electoral programs and FEC support/oppose filings, not a blank formal party field. Democratic-leaning can include cross-party endorsements; documented bipartisan programs stay separate. Per-record hash-bound reviews retain original classifications and do not rewrite text or extraction evidence.
  • Unresolved and other publisher alignments remain in the evidence explorer and downloads but do not enter Democratic/Republican comparison denominators.
  • Charts count unique exact messages per party after filters, not placements, spending or reach.
  • Issue charts show exact phrase-family matches, not validated semantic topic classifications.
  • Matched language may be quoted, denied, or used positively. It is not an attack classification.
  • Opponent examples are editorially context-checked excerpts, not representative estimates.
  • Party/chamber/source composition differs. Use Senate or within-race filters for closer comparisons.
  • Unknown election phase and race remain unknown rather than inferred solely from calendar dates.
  • Ads make claims. This project does not establish their truth or measure persuasion.
  • Automatic video captions may misrecognize words or names. On-screen-only claims are not yet included in caption-only records.
  • Reviewed campaign originals retain exact publisher post timestamps and media hashes. A post date is not verified first release or paid delivery; undated recovered originals stay outside dated comparisons. Local speech recognition may misrecognize names or negation.
  • Publisher-quoted ad copy is separately flagged: it may omit parts of the creative. Press commentary and biographies are excluded.
  • Reviewed standalone image ads use local OCR with image hashes, line coordinates, and explicit word/reading-order uncertainty; video thumbnails are excluded.
  • Google native image/search previews are joined by exact public FEC committee IDs. Each preview stays separate; parent-creative delivery does not prove each variant served.
  • Google previews include fundraising and payer/platform boilerplate and may truncate text. Compare formats separately; their prevalence is not a party-strategy finding.
  • Temporal charts anchor pre-2026 Google carryovers to January's first in-scope date, not a new launch. First/last delivery intervals do not establish continuous delivery.
  • Snap payers use exact FEC IDs, exact FEC legal names or explicitly reviewed name crosswalks. Repeated platform ad IDs sharing a payer and asset form one creative record.
  • Native Snap videos use local multilingual Whisper speech recognition and one-second frame OCR. Words, names and short overlays may be missed or misrecognized; none is a certified transcript.
  • Speech and novel on-screen lines are separate evidence lanes. Repeated speech/subtitles are deduplicated, low-confidence OCR is retained outside analyzed text, and phrase matches cannot cross from speech into an overlay or between independent overlay lines.
